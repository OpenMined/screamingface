# ABC Thesis Citations — Cohort Analysis
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://attribution-based-control.ai/ — thesis by Andrew Trask

---

## About This Cohort

The researchers cited in Andrew Trask's "Attribution-Based Control in AI Systems" form a specific intellectual lineage: cryptographers who built the privacy-preserving math the thesis depends on, AI safety researchers who shaped the governance framing, legal scholars who are litigating the attribution problem in court, and public intellectuals who are translating all of it into policy language and democratic argument. What distinguishes this cohort from a general AI influencer list is that they aren't famous for fame's sake — they're cited because their work is load-bearing for the thesis's core claims. That gives screamingface something to work with: a genuine intellectual relationship to each person, not a cold outreach.

---

## Methodology

**Source material:** Full citation list from attribution-based-control.ai (references.html), supplemented by web research for each prioritized individual. Research conducted March 2026.

**Priority process:** The full citation list was filtered to individual researchers (removing institutional, government, and anonymous citations), then prioritized through two lenses: first, Andrew Trask's likely technical and intellectual affinities based on the thesis and the screamingface devplan; then reviewed and rebalanced by a policy-and-public-reach lens to weight for Audience 2 goals (amplification, coalition-building, and policy influence) over technical credibility alone.

**Framing:** Reactions to screamingface are synthesized from each person's known positions and public record. They are informed approximations, not verified statements. No one on this list was contacted. These are directional reads for positioning and prioritization, not for attribution.

## How This Cohort Divides

The 13 prioritized researchers fall into four groups based on what they bring to the Audience 2 engagement strategy.

---

### Group 1 — Governance Architects (Dafoe, Garfinkel)

**Who they are:** The two co-authors of the Structured Transparency paper with Trask. Allan Dafoe founded GovAI and now leads AI safety and governance at DeepMind. Ben Garfinkel directs GovAI. Both think about AI governance as an institutional design problem — how do you build coordination mechanisms that hold under strategic pressure? — and both have existing intellectual relationships with the thesis itself.

**Their orientation to screamingface:** The warmest possible entry point. They already co-signed the foundational ideas. The question isn't whether they're sympathetic but whether screamingface demonstrates that the theory works in practice. If it does, they're natural co-authors for policy papers, cited validators in governance documents, and connectors to the institutional network GovAI has built. Dafoe in particular bridges directly to the international relations and state-level coordination conversations that will matter as the 2-year window closes.

**What they need:** Evidence, not pitch. They'll want to see whether the attribution mechanism holds up to scrutiny and whether the governance properties claimed for the ensemble are real.

---

### Group 2 — Public Intellectuals with Reach (Schneier, Véliz, Pasquale, Russell, Summerfield)

**Who they are:** Five people who have each written general-audience books on AI power, privacy, or democracy — and have proven audiences for those arguments. Schneier blogs to hundreds of thousands; Véliz's *Privacy Is Power* was Economist Book of the Year; Pasquale's *Black Box Society* is on congressional staffers' reading lists; Russell delivered the BBC Reith Lectures; Summerfield wrote *These Strange New Minds* for Penguin and holds a seat at the UK AI Security Institute.

**Their orientation to screamingface:** Each arrives through a different door — Schneier through security architecture, Véliz through data rights, Pasquale through antitrust, Russell through AI controllability, Summerfield through democratic epistemics — but all five arrive at the same structural interest: an open ensemble that routes around concentrated AI is the kind of infrastructure they've been arguing for in the abstract. The concrete proof is what they've been missing.

**What they need:** A clear, non-technical explanation of how the architecture addresses the concentration problem, and a honest answer to their specific objection (Schneier: does open routing actually distribute power if the models are still proprietary? Véliz: what does screamingface itself collect? Pasquale: does this substitute for regulation or complement it? Russell: can the ensemble be formally audited? Summerfield: how does this reach non-developers?).

---

### Group 3 — Legal & Attribution Specialists (Samuelson, Gabriel)

**Who they are:** Pamela Samuelson at Berkeley Law and Iason Gabriel at DeepMind approach the problem from opposite directions — one from IP law and copyright, the other from value alignment philosophy — but both are centrally concerned with the attribution question that is also the thesis's central technical claim.

**Their orientation to screamingface:** Samuelson would engage with the chain of custody question: if screamingface tracks which model generated which output, does that help resolve the copyright liability questions that courts are currently trying to answer through doctrine? Gabriel would engage with the values question: whose values does the ensemble optimize for when models disagree, and is that decision auditable? Both represent entry points into communities (copyright law, AI alignment) that have direct stakes in whether attribution-based control works.

**What they need:** Specifics. Both are precise thinkers who will see through vague claims. Samuelson needs to know about model training data provenance in the ensemble. Gabriel needs to know how routing decisions are made and documented.

---

### Group 4 — Technical Validators (Dwork, Papernot, McMahan)

**Who they are:** The inventors and leading researchers of differential privacy (Dwork), privacy-preserving ML and ensemble methods (Papernot), and federated learning (McMahan). Their work is the technical substrate the ABC thesis is built on. They're not natural public advocates, and none of them are going to write a blog post about screamingface — but their technical sign-off carries exceptional weight with the researchers, policymakers, and grant-makers who fund the infrastructure the thesis calls for.

**Their orientation to screamingface:** Narrow and technical. Dwork will ask whether the privacy math holds under composition. Papernot will immediately notice the structural parallel to PATE and ask whether the routing layer has been evaluated against adversarial threat models. McMahan will want to understand whether the ensemble architecture is compatible with proper federated training at scale. These are not PR conversations; they're audits. If screamingface passes them, the credibility gain is significant.

**What they need:** Formal documentation of the privacy and attribution claims — not marketing language, actual analysis.

## What This Means for screamingface

### The thesis is the introduction

Every person on this list has a concrete intellectual reason to pay attention to screamingface that doesn't require cold outreach. Dafoe and Garfinkel co-wrote the foundational paper. Dwork, Papernot, and McMahan are cited because their work makes the architecture possible. Russell, Bengio, Schneier, Véliz, Pasquale, Samuelson, Summerfield, and Gabriel are cited because their framing of the AI power problem is exactly what screamingface is positioned to address. The thesis is a letter of introduction that most external projects don't have.

### Four distinct entry narratives — pick the door for the audience

Different people in the Audience 2 space are motivated by different frames. This cohort maps cleanly onto four distinct narratives that each have their own champions:

- **Control & safety** (Russell, Bengio): "No single company should control AI inference."
- **Privacy & data rights** (Schneier, Véliz, Dwork): "Distributing queries across providers is structural privacy, not just a setting."
- **Copyright & attribution** (Samuelson, Gabriel): "The ensemble tracks attribution — which matters for the legal and values questions courts and ethicists are actively fighting over."
- **Democracy & power** (Pasquale, Summerfield, Dafoe, Garfinkel): "AI monoculture is a democratic infrastructure problem and this is a concrete technical intervention."

### The 2-year window is this cohort's frame, not a new argument

Most of these researchers have been making the "window is closing" argument in their own domains — Bengio on civilizational risk, Pasquale on antitrust, Summerfield on democratic epistemics, Russell on AI controllability. Screamingface doesn't need to convince them the problem is real; it needs to show them that something is actually being built while the window is open.

### The validation sequence matters

Not everyone on this list should be approached at the same time or in the same way. The technical validators (Group 4) should be engaged for audit and formal review. The governance architects (Group 1) can co-author. The public intellectuals (Group 2) amplify once there's something credible to amplify. The legal/values specialists (Group 3) are ideal for specific collaborative outputs (a joint statement on attribution, a law review piece on AI routing and copyright). The sequence: validate → co-author → amplify.
