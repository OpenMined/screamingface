# Allan Dafoe
**Role:** Director of Frontier Safety and Governance, Google DeepMind; Founder and former President, Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI)
**Category:** ABC Citations — P1

## Who They Are
Allan Dafoe is one of the most influential academic architects of the AI governance field, having founded GovAI at Oxford before moving to Google DeepMind where he leads work on AI safety and international cooperation. He co-authored "Beyond Privacy Trade-offs with Structured Transparency" with Andrew Trask, which positions privacy-preserving computation not as a cost but as an institutional design opportunity. His framework treats AI governance as a problem of international relations — how do states, institutions, and firms coordinate under conditions of strategic uncertainty and rapid capability growth?

## Relationship to AI Tools
A researcher and institutional builder rather than a practitioner. Dafoe engages with AI tools primarily through the lens of governance design: who controls them, how they're audited, what coordination problems they create or solve. He's attuned to the difference between technical possibility and institutional reality — he knows that even well-designed systems fail if the governance structures around them aren't built.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Dafoe would engage with screamingface primarily as a case study in decentralized AI governance rather than as a tool. The ensemble routing architecture — where no single provider holds the full context of a user's work — maps directly onto his structured transparency research. He'd be interested in whether the attribution mechanism could serve as a template for broader AI accountability infrastructure, and likely skeptical of whether an open-source CLI project can maintain decentralization as it scales. He might cite it in policy writing as evidence that technical alternatives to concentrated AI exist, while questioning whether the incentive structures hold long-term.

## Key Tension
Can a developer-facing CLI tool that depends on API access to the very companies it routes around serve as genuine infrastructure for decentralized AI, or does it remain permanently vulnerable to the coordination failures it was designed to prevent?
