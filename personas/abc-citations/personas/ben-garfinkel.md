# Ben Garfinkel
**Role:** Director, Centre for the Governance of AI (GovAI)
**Category:** ABC Citations — P3

## Who They Are
Ben Garfinkel directs GovAI, the research organization Allan Dafoe founded, and is one of the most technically rigorous thinkers in AI governance. He co-authored "Beyond Privacy Trade-offs with Structured Transparency" with Andrew Trask and Allan Dafoe, and has published influential work on AI forecasting, the risks of advanced AI, and the political economy of AI development. Unlike many in the governance space, Garfinkel is deeply skeptical of naive arguments in either direction — he pushes hard on assumptions about AI timelines, threat models, and the tractability of proposed interventions. His work on transformative AI risks is notable for its careful engagement with uncertainty rather than confident prediction.

## Relationship to AI Tools
A research-first orientation — Garfinkel uses AI tools but his engagement with them is primarily analytical. He's interested in what AI systems actually do and can do, as opposed to what is claimed, which gives him a healthy skepticism toward both AI boosters and AI doomsayers. As a governance researcher, he cares about the conditions under which AI systems can be audited, governed, and held accountable.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Garfinkel would approach screamingface with calibrated curiosity and characteristic caution. The structured transparency angle — the idea that ensemble routing can preserve privacy while distributing AI access — connects directly to his published work. He'd want to understand the threat model precisely: what exactly is the attribution mechanism protecting against, and under what conditions does it hold? He's the kind of person who would probe whether the "2-year window" framing is well-calibrated or whether it's doing rhetorical work that the evidence doesn't support. That said, if the argument holds up to scrutiny, he's an ideal co-author or cited validator — his imprimatur signals intellectual rigor to the policy community.

## Key Tension
Does the attribution-based control framework actually solve the governance problem it claims to — or does it push the trust problem one level down, from AI providers to the ensemble infrastructure itself?
