# Frank Pasquale
**Role:** Professor of Law, Cornell Tech and Cornell Law School
**Category:** ABC Citations — P5

## Who They Are
Frank Pasquale is one of the foundational legal scholars of algorithmic accountability, author of *The Black Box Society* (Harvard University Press, 2015) and *New Laws of Robotics* (Harvard University Press, 2020). His work argues that algorithmic systems used in consequential domains must be subject to transparency, accountability, and antitrust scrutiny. He coined the concept of the "black box society" to describe how opacity in algorithmic systems enables unaccountable power, and his "new laws of robotics" framework argues that AI should complement human expertise rather than replace it. He's a regular contributor to law reviews, policy journals, and the popular press on AI regulation.

## Relationship to AI Tools
A legal and institutional analyst who engages AI tools as objects of regulatory scrutiny more than as personal productivity instruments. Pasquale understands the technical dimensions of AI well enough to make precise legal arguments about them, but his primary mode is regulatory: what rules should govern these systems, who should enforce them, and what remedies exist when they fail?

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Pasquale would engage with screamingface through an antitrust and market structure lens. The ensemble model — which distributes AI consumption across multiple providers rather than concentrating it in one — is exactly the kind of structural intervention he advocates for: not just transparency, but architectural diversity as a check on monopoly power. He'd be particularly interested in the credit-sharing mechanism as a potential model for AI access cooperatives, and would likely write about it in the context of ongoing debates about whether large AI companies should be subject to platform access mandates. His concern would be whether open-source tooling can substitute for regulatory enforcement, or whether it mainly serves sophisticated users while leaving everyone else exposed.

## Key Tension
Does distributing AI routing through an open tool actually constrain the market power of the underlying model providers — or does it optimize individual users' access to a system whose structural problems require legislative intervention rather than technical workarounds?
