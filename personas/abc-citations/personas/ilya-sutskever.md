# Ilya Sutskever
**Role:** Co-founder and CEO, Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI); Co-founder and former Chief Scientist, OpenAI
**Category:** ABC Citations — P17

## Who They Are
Ilya Sutskever is one of the most technically influential figures in modern deep learning, co-author of the landmark ImageNet paper (Krizhevsky, Sutskever, Hinton 2012) that catalyzed the current era of neural network research, and co-founder of OpenAI where he served as Chief Scientist for nearly a decade. After a high-profile departure from OpenAI in mid-2024 -- preceded by his involvement in the board's brief removal of Sam Altman -- he founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), a company structured around a single goal: building safe superintelligence with no commercial products, no API platform, and no distractions. His core thesis is that alignment must be solved completely before deployment, and that commercial pressures inevitably corrupt safety research.

## Relationship to AI Tools
Sutskever is a builder at the deepest architectural level -- not a tool user in the conventional sense but someone who shapes the foundations that tools like Claude, GPT, and Gemini are built on. He co-led OpenAI's Superalignment project before leaving, and his current work at SSI operates in deliberate secrecy, releasing nothing until superintelligence can be deployed safely. He views incremental product-oriented AI development as structurally misaligned with the safety problem.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Sutskever would regard screamingface as an interesting engineering layer operating at the wrong level of abstraction for the problems he cares about. The ensemble routing concept -- combining multiple frontier models to beat any single model's benchmarks -- would strike him as clever optimization within a paradigm he considers transitional. He has publicly argued that the current era of static pre-training and scaling is ending, and that fundamentally new approaches to learning and generalization are needed. The attribution-based control framework underlying screamingface might draw more genuine interest: the idea that inference decisions should be transparent, auditable, and distributed across providers aligns with his insistence that AI power concentration is the core danger. But he would likely view it as a governance patch on systems that need architectural redesign, not a path toward the kind of intrinsic safety he is pursuing at SSI.

## Key Tension
If the current generation of frontier models represents a transitional paradigm that Sutskever believes will be superseded, does an ensemble system built to optimize across those models invest in infrastructure with a short shelf life -- or does the open, auditable routing layer outlast the models it orchestrates?
