# Nicolas Papernot
**Role:** Associate Professor, University of Toronto (ECE, CS, Law); Canada CIFAR AI Chair, Vector Institute; Co-Director, Canadian AI Safety Institute (CAISI) Research Program at CIFAR; International Chair, Inria (2025–2027)
**Category:** ABC Citations — P12

## Who They Are
Nicolas Papernot works on the intersection of machine learning security, privacy-preserving computation, and trustworthy AI — and his institutional footprint now spans all three of academic research, industry collaboration (Google DeepMind), and government policy (CAISI). His PATE framework (Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles) is structurally notable in this context: it achieves strong privacy guarantees by aggregating predictions from an ensemble of models trained on partitioned data, which is architecturally analogous to what screamingface does at the prompt routing level. Winner of the 2025 Steacie Prize. His joint appointment in the Law faculty at Toronto and his CAISI co-directorship make him a rare figure who can translate technical proof into policy argument with credibility in both directions.

## Relationship to AI Tools
A builder and security researcher. Papernot builds privacy-preserving ML systems and then attacks them — he has published extensively on adversarial examples, model extraction, and the ways that privacy properties degrade under realistic threat models. He'd approach screamingface as a security auditor before an endorser.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Papernot would immediately recognize the structural parallel between PATE and ensemble routing. He'd be technically engaged but forensic — his first questions would be about the threat model: what are the adversarial conditions under which the routing privacy breaks down, and has the system been evaluated against them? He'd want to know whether the attribution mechanism provides formal guarantees or heuristic ones. If the architecture is sound, he's an ideal technical validator whose credibility spans academia, industry, and the Canadian policy community — a particularly valuable combination for the public-sector coalition the project needs to build.

## Key Tension
Does ensemble prompt routing achieve the privacy guarantees it implies — and has it been evaluated against the adversarial conditions (model extraction, membership inference, routing manipulation) that Papernot's research shows are realistic attack surfaces?
