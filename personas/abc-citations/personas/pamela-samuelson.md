# Pamela Samuelson
**Role:** Richard M. Sherman Distinguished Professor of Law and Information, UC Berkeley School of Law and School of Information; Co-Director, Berkeley Center for Law & Technology; Board Chair, Electronic Frontier Foundation; Co-founder and Board Chair, Authors Alliance
**Category:** ABC Citations — P7

## Who They Are
Pamela Samuelson is the preeminent legal scholar at the intersection of intellectual property, digital technology, and the public interest. For thirty years she has shaped how copyright law applies to software, the internet, and now generative AI — her 2023 *Science* article "Generative AI Meets Copyright" became a foundational reference in AI legal discourse within months of publication. She co-founded Authors Alliance to advance the rights of authors who want their work widely accessible rather than locked up, putting her in an unusual position: defending creators while also defending the public's right to use and build on creative work. Her EFF board seat and her academic record both reflect a consistent view that IP maximalism stifles innovation and that legal frameworks should serve the public interest, not incumbents.

## Relationship to AI Tools
Samuelson engages with AI primarily as a legal analyst and policy architect, not a practitioner. She tracks AI through the lens of copyright law, training data rights, and fair use doctrine. She is neither a technophobe nor a booster — she evaluates systems by whether their legal and institutional design serves the public or concentrates rights and power in a small number of actors.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Samuelson's entry point would be training data and model provenance, not ensemble routing. She would immediately ask: what data trained each model in the ensemble, and does routing queries through open models implicate different copyright exposure than routing through commercial APIs? The attribution mechanism — tracking which model contributed what — would interest her precisely because attribution is the linchpin of the copyright cases she's been analyzing. If screamingface can demonstrate a chain of custody for AI-generated outputs, it may offer a technical partial solution to the attribution problem that courts are currently trying to resolve through doctrine. Her concern: if a user runs an Ollama model trained on unlicensed data, does the routing layer insulate or implicate the user?

## Key Tension
Screamingface's decentralized architecture could advance the legal accountability and attribution transparency that Samuelson argues AI systems need — but only if the open-source models in the ensemble were trained on legitimately licensed data, which for most current open-weight models remains an open and actively litigated question.
