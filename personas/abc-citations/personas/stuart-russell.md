# Stuart Russell
**Role:** Professor of Computer Science (Smith-Zadeh Chair in Engineering), UC Berkeley; Founder and Director, Center for Human-Compatible Artificial Intelligence (CHAI); Co-Chair, OECD Expert Group on AI Futures
**Category:** ABC Citations — P6

## Who They Are
Stuart Russell is among the most cited active AI researchers and co-author of *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, the field's standard textbook now in its fourth edition. His 2019 book *Human Compatible* reframed the AI control problem: the risk isn't malevolent AI but AI optimizing confidently for the wrong objectives, and the fix requires building systems that remain uncertain about human preferences and actively seek to be corrected. He delivered the BBC Reith Lectures in 2021, was elected Fellow of the Royal Society in 2025, and named a Time 100 AI Shaper that same year — recognition that his influence spans technical research, public communication, and international governance simultaneously.

## Relationship to AI Tools
Russell is a builder and a critic at the same institutional moment — a rare combination. He designs AI systems and also convenes the governance bodies meant to constrain them. He chairs the OECD Expert Group on AI Futures and engages with the World Economic Forum's AI governance work. His engagement with tools is principled: does this system remain correctable? Does it concentrate or distribute power over inference about human values?

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
Russell would engage with screamingface not as a product but as a structural intervention. The ensemble routing architecture — no single model holds the full relationship with the user, no single company controls the inference pipeline — maps directly onto his "machine uncertainty" framework: a system that distributes AI capability across multiple providers is architecturally less likely to lock in a single objective function or a single corporate preference model. He'd likely cite it favorably in policy contexts as evidence that decentralized AI infrastructure is technically feasible, not theoretical. His concern would be about provability: can the ensemble's behavior be formally verified as beneficial, or is it just stochastically better? He'd push for the kind of audit trail and attribution mechanism that makes the system's decisions legible to governance bodies, not just users.

## Key Tension
Screamingface routes around concentrated AI providers, which Russell would endorse in principle — but his framework demands *provably* beneficial systems, and an open ensemble whose routing decisions can't be formally audited may trade one opacity for another.
