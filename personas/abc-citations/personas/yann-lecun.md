# Yann LeCun
**Role:** VP and Chief AI Scientist, Meta; Silver Professor, New York University
**Category:** ABC Citations — P15

## Who They Are
Yann LeCun is a Turing Award winner (2018, shared with Bengio and Hinton) and the architect of convolutional neural networks, which became foundational to modern deep learning. His co-authored 2015 "Deep Learning" review in Nature with Bengio and Hinton is one of the most cited papers in the field and is referenced in the ABC thesis. At Meta, he leads fundamental AI research while simultaneously serving as the loudest, most combative public advocate for open-source AI — arguing on social media with unusual frequency and directness that open models are safer, more beneficial, and more scientifically productive than closed ones.

## Relationship to AI Tools
LeCun is a builder at the institutional scale. He oversees the research behind Meta's LLaMA model family, which Meta releases with open weights. He engages with AI tools not as an end user but as someone shaping what the tools are at the architectural level. His public intellectual energy goes toward fighting what he sees as the twin threats of AI doomerism and corporate lock-in — positions he defends daily on X/Twitter with a bluntness unusual for someone at his seniority.

## Likely Reaction to screamingface
LeCun would approve of the structural premise: an open routing layer that treats models as interchangeable components rather than walled gardens. The ensemble approach aligns with his long-standing argument that no single architecture or company should monopolize AI capability. He'd particularly appreciate that screamingface includes Ollama (local open-weight models) alongside commercial APIs — this validates the idea that open models belong in the same competitive tier. Where he'd push back is on the safety framing. LeCun has been explicitly dismissive of the premise that current AI systems pose existential risk, and the ABC thesis's emphasis on control and attribution might read to him as conceding too much to the "AI safety" crowd he publicly spars with. He'd want the tool sold on capability and openness, not on governance and risk mitigation.

## Key Tension
If open-weight models like LLaMA are already safe by virtue of being open and auditable, does screamingface's attribution-based control layer add genuine technical value — or does it implicitly validate the "AI is dangerous" framing LeCun has spent years publicly dismantling?
