# Persona Weighting Guide

This is the routing doc for persona-informed work. Consult this before any copy, design, positioning, or feature work to know which audiences matter for the task at hand.

If a task doesn't clearly map to a context below, ask which audience to target.

---

## Audiences

### Audience 1 — Technical Developers & Benchmark Enthusiasts
**Priority:** P0 (primary launch target)
**File:** `personas/persona-audience-1.md`

The people who will actually use screamingface. Software engineers, ML practitioners, AI-adjacent developers. They run models, compare benchmarks, and close tabs on marketing BS in five seconds. Build for them first. Prove it works, then they tell everyone.

### Audience 2 — AI/Society Thought Leaders, Journalists & Policy Champions
**Priority:** P1 (engage now, build toward)
**File:** `personas/persona-audience-2.md`

The people who shape how AI is understood outside technical circles. Journalists, researchers, policy thinkers. They amplify, validate, and unlock coalitions and public funding. They need a different front door than Audience 1 — editorial, principled, non-technical.

### Core Team — OpenMined Internal
**Priority:** Pre-launch only
**File:** `personas/openmined-core-team.md`

The ~32-person OpenMined team. First real users, internal beta audience. Use this persona for internal review and dogfooding. Once the product launches externally, Audience 1 and 2 take over as primary guides.

### ABC Cohort — Peers & Validators
**Priority:** Reference (ongoing)
**Report:** `personas/abc-citations/abc-citations-report.md`
**Individual profiles:** `personas/abc-citations/personas/*.md`

Researchers cited in Andrew Trask's thesis at https://attribution-based-control.ai/. These are not a traditional "audience" — they are potential peers, co-authors, and validators with a pre-existing intellectual relationship to screamingface's foundations. Some align with Audience 2 (public intellectuals, policy thinkers). Others are technical validators who would audit the claims. Individual profiles should indicate whether each person is primarily a **peer** (collaborator/validator) or **Audience 2** (amplifier/policy champion). Read the group report for general orientation; dive into individual profiles only when doing targeted work.

### Time 100 AI — Reference Cohort
**Priority:** Reference (Audience 2-adjacent)
**Report:** `personas/time100-ai/persona-time-100-ai-report.md`
**Individual profiles:** `personas/time100-ai/personas/{thinkers,shapers,leaders,innovators}/*.md`

TIME's 100 most influential people in AI (2025). Most connected to Audience 2 considerations — they are the voices that shape public understanding of AI. The report organizes them into five groups by orientation. Read the group report for positioning context; dive into individual profiles only when targeting specific people or sub-groups.

Use this table to determine which personas to prioritize for a given task.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Homepage (/)** | Audience 1 | Audience 2 | — |
| **/why page** | Audience 2 | — | ABC report, Time 100 report |
| **App screens** | Audience 1 | — | — |

---

## How to Use This in Practice

1. **Before any copy, design, or positioning work**, check this table. Know who you're writing for.
2. **Read the primary audience's persona file** to get tone, messaging do's/don'ts, and proof points.
3. **If a secondary audience is listed**, scan their persona for conflicts — make sure you're not alienating them.
4. **Reference cohorts** (ABC, Time 100) are for deeper context, not routine use. Read the group reports when:
   - Working on the /why page or societal framing
   - Positioning for policy/press audiences
   - Answering "how would [specific person/group] react to this?"
5. **If the task doesn't fit the table**, ask which audience to target before proceeding. Don't guess.
6. **Individual profiles** (the per-person files in ABC and Time 100) are for targeted work only — writing about or positioning for a specific person or sub-group. Don't load them all for general tasks.
